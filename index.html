If the download stalls at 100%
You are probably in an in-app browser (opened from Gmail, a text, or a chat app). Those often hang on app downloads. Tap the menu (⋮) and choose Open in Chrome browser, then download again.

Installing

  1. Open the downloaded file. Android will warn you about an unknown source — that is expected for an app installed outside the Play Store. Allow it for your browser.
  2. If you already have the app, Android offers Update. Take it — it keeps your settings and your history. Do not uninstall first, that erases both.

Read this before you use it

This app trades Kalshi's 15-minute Bitcoin markets. It is a testing instrument, not a money maker. Every strategy preset in it has been measured over 160 markets and every one won between 48% and 52% of the time on data it had not seen — a coin flip, and fees make a coin flip a slow loss.

What it is genuinely good at is letting you test an idea honestly: a paper mode that runs the same code as live against real prices, real fee math in every stake, and a recorder that logs every market to a CSV you can analyse yourself.

Start on paper and stay there for days. Markets roll every 15 minutes, so one day is about 96 markets. You need several hundred before a result means anything. A winning afternoon is not a result — presets in this app have made money for 35 hours and then given it all back.

When you move to real money

  • You need your own Kalshi account and your own API key. Keys are stored encrypted on your phone and are never sent anywhere except to Kalshi.
  • Set "Give up after this many losses" to 1. That is flat staking: a loss is just a loss. Higher values raise the stake to chase losses, and on a small account a losing streak empties it.
  • Kalshi splits your balance internally, and an order can only spend the pocket it routes to. Your usable size is the biggest single pocket, not your balance.
  • Risk no more than 3–5% of your balance per bet. It will feel too small. That is the point.

This is not financial or trading advice. You are responsible for your own trades. Automated trading can lose money faster than you can watch it, and an outage, a bug, or a dropped connection can cost you real money.

Sending feedback

The HUD tab has Share log, and the Ledger tab has Export research data (CSV). Those two are far more useful than a description of what went wrong. Neither one contains your API keys.
